The Game of Pretend

Prompt Engineering & In-Context Learning

Meet Your Super-Smart Robot Friend!

Imagine this robot has read almost every book in the world. It knows a lot! But there's a catch:

It doesn't automatically know how you want it to act unless you tell it.

What is Prompt Engineering?

The Old Way

Opening up the robot's brain with a screwdriver to change how it works (reprogramming/fine-tuning).

VS

Prompt Engineering

Using our words to set the rules of the game. Giving the robot the best possible instructions!

Step 1 of 3

1. System (The Main Rule)

This is where you tell the robot who it is supposed to be.

The "Game of Pretend"

"You are a grumpy pirate captain who loves treasure."

The Tech Reality

{"role": "system", "content": "You are an expert code reviewer."}
Step 2 of 3

2. Context (The Practice Round)

Sometimes, telling isn't enough. We need to show them how to play. This is called In-Context Learning (Few-Shot).

Pirate Practice Round:

If I say 'Hello'...
...you say 'Arrrgh, go away!'
If I say 'Look, a shiny coin!'...
...you say 'Mine! Give me the loot!'

The robot learns the pattern right then and there!

Geek Out Moment

How does the Robot "Learn" in Context?

Under the hood, Large Language Models (LLMs) are just predicting the next word (token). By providing examples, you change the conditional probability of the next word.

Step 3 of 3

3. User (Your Turn!)

This is where you finally ask the robot your real question or give it a task.

"Look at this cute puppy!"

Robot thinking based on rules & examples...

"Arrrgh, keep that furry beast away from me ship's wheel!"

Why is this so cool?

No New Robot Needed

You didn't build a new Pirate Robot. You used the same general robot, but gave it a specific game to play.

Instant Switching

Want to play "Astronaut" instead? Just change the System prompt and Examples. It switches immediately!
